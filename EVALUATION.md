# Nightingale: failure-first build assessment

**Recommendation:** Ship a synthetic-data vertical slice for evaluation; **do not place real patients on it next week**. The trust-critical path is working in this repository, but identity, clinical safety, privacy assurance, platform permissions and operational response are not yet production-ready. Calling this “clinic-ready” would be the most consequential failure.

## Simplified scope and reason

I prioritized one coherent path: attributed arrival → anonymous value → consented conversion → risk-gated patient chat → versioned facts → human queue. It is stronger evidence than a broad but disconnected dashboard. For required channels, the prototype supports `staff_referral`, `social_comment`, `instagram_ad_click` and `website_widget`; it also has configurable simulations for Google ad clicks, lead forms and Google reviews. All use a single channel configuration, not scattered opening rules. The staff referral link resumes the **same** LeadSession and carries its preloaded topic.

The nurse-facing **Channel & anonymous inquiries** view exposes source channel, campaign, arrival time and guest message count after a conversation, while withholding contact details and chat content until the visitor explicitly chooses to share and completes consent. The visitor's **Share my contact details** button opens a phone-and-email form. If a visitor types a recognized identifier before that step, the page warns them and the application persists only the redacted message, immediately rather than waiting ten minutes. This is a demo-level guarantee about application storage, not a claim that an imperfect detector can identify all personal information or erase network/intermediary copies. A production ten-minute deletion SLA would need auditable storage inventory, cache/log controls and deletion verification.

The nurse's **Funnel by channel** view counts distinct LeadSessions at each recorded event and shows adjacent-stage drop-offs, so a channel with strong arrival but weak authentication is visible. A separate `clinical_intent` count keeps urgent guests out of the “value delivered” bucket; qualification combines value or clinical intent for an honest path to authentication. **Prioritized leads** use a disclosed additive score—arrival recency, source channel, consented identity, and funnel stage. Risk is a separate, dominant care-priority flag, not a sales-score multiplier. Anonymous entries reveal neither contact details nor conversation-derived concern; after consent, the top concern comes from the provenance-linked patient profile. A guest clinical concern enters the nurse queue only after the patient explicitly consents to sharing. Care follow-up is suggested only for consented, non-clinical entries; marketing still requires distinct consent.

The pre-signup value is a factual service answer or a question-preparation prompt from a small local source catalog, or a user-controlled draft of at most 240 characters. Specific questions about cost, process, success and risks have distinct grounded answers; when the clinic has supplied no verified price or live appointment feed, the assistant says so and offers useful next questions instead of inventing facts. Each substantive answer records a `value_event`. The weekly statistic counts **distinct guest conversations started** via live SQL and stays hidden below five; it does not pretend anonymous sessions are unique people or invent a number. The draft uses safe, general wording rather than making an unsupported patient-specific medical claim, and is never posted automatically. The UI does **not** send a personalized pre-signup email. That would require a PHI-safe renderer, transactional mail provider, delivery/abuse controls, and a separate marketing-consent record. The schema already timestamps marketing consent, but no marketing send is implemented.

The anonymous/protected-PWA requirement is internally tense: the brief asks for useful anonymous access and also says users must authenticate before accessing the PWA. I interpret this as anonymous access to a limited guest surface, with authentication required for patient records and staff tools. The application follows that interpretation; the clinic should confirm it before deployment.

## Channel feasibility and identity boundary

Meta's official Instagram API materials describe comment management, private replies and the permissions required for professional accounts; normal Send API messaging has recipient-initiated conversation constraints. Thus a comment should be handled through the platform's specific private-reply mechanism, **not** an assumption that the clinic can freely DM any commenter. A real connector needs an approved professional account, app review/permissions, webhook validation, replay protection and a tested one-reply policy. [Meta Instagram API collection](https://www.postman.com/meta/instagram/documentation/6yqw8pt/instagram-api?entity=request-23987686-4b9737aa-d320-498a-a092-7225a0a785b7), [Meta Instagram workspace](https://www.postman.com/meta/instagram/overview).

TikTok's public developer webhook event list does not by itself establish a general comment-to-DM capability. Its Business API documentation describes Comment-to-Message settings, so access must be checked for the clinic's specific business account and region before promising this route. [TikTok developer webhook events](https://developers.tiktok.com/docs/en/webhooks-events), [TikTok Business API](https://business-api.tiktok.com/gateway/docs/index?doc_id=1735713875563521). A **like** is not evidence that the person invited a health-related contact; this build never initiates contact from likes. For anonymous Google Reviews, a public reply can carry a general “ask us” link, but it must not disclose or infer a health concern. Google's API allows review replies for verified locations. [Google Business Profile review replies](https://developers.google.com/my-business/reference/rest/v4/accounts.locations.reviews/updateReply).

In every path, `identity_level` is explicit: anonymous click, handle-only comment, or volunteered email. An email from a lead form is not the same as a verified login. The immutable internal user ID, rather than email or phone, anchors longitudinal history.

## Clinical, privacy and operational gates

The model should never be the first risk gate. For every patient turn: store the message securely; persist a timestamped risk assessment linked to it; run deterministic emergency, uncertainty and personal-treatment-request checks; if medium/high, withhold medical advice and persist escalation; only for low risk consider grounded education with a citation. The patient's thread shows the level, reason, response confidence and time, and the nurse's escalation payload includes the assessment. Even then, an LLM timeout, ungrounded citation or failed redaction should yield a safe fallback and a human option. The current prototype uses deterministic FAQ responses, so its citations resolve to local source spans and no raw data leaves for a model. It is honest to call this a **conversation prototype**, not a clinically safe AI deployment.

The brief names names, IC/ID numbers and phones. These are a minimum redaction test set, not a complete privacy standard. US HHS guidance illustrates that names, dates, locations, emails, medical-record numbers, URLs, IP addresses and other identifiers in free text can also identify a person; Singapore's PDPC provides healthcare-sector guidance on collection, use and disclosure. Neither source makes a simple regex sufficient. Before any external LLM use, the clinic needs a jurisdiction-specific data-flow review, broader detection with measured recall, manual adversarial evaluation, an agreement with the model provider, and a hard stop when de-identification is uncertain. [HHS de-identification guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html), [Singapore PDPC healthcare guidance](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/guidelines-and-consultation/2017/10/advisory-guidelines-for-the-healthcare-sector).

Operational behavior must be explicit:

- **LLM timeout or malformed answer:** return a bounded general fallback, do not invent a citation, preserve the patient's message, and offer human review. The current local FAQ has no external LLM dependency.
- **Redaction failure or uncertainty:** do not call the external model. Encrypt and retain the message under the applicable consent state; show a safe explanation and route for human review. The current detector cannot reliably detect its own misses, so enabling an external model is blocked.
- **Authentication outage:** do not convert the LeadSession or expose patient history. Keep an encrypted guest session within the three-day window and allow the user to resume verification later.
- **Queue delivery or clinic outage:** do not show “sent” until a durable escalation record exists. Record failed notification attempts and surface an alternate contact route. The demo persists the record but has no real delivery monitor.

For urgent symptoms, “response in 12–18 hours” cannot be the primary promise. Show emergency instructions immediately and separately. The 12–18 hour estimate should apply only to ordinary clinic review, after the clinic confirms its staffing pattern. The requested “999” wording is present, but the clinic must validate the emergency wording and geographic applicability.

## What the tests establish—and what they do not

Run `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v`. The suite proves the specified guest conversion, attribution and original-message provenance; live value count and local citation span; persisted escalation payload; mandatory high-risk phrases and ambiguous chest symptom; medication mutation with both source pointers; named-person/IC/phone redaction in the tested examples and PHI-free audit logs; patient isolation and staff queue access; and an honest answer to “Are you a real doctor?” It also checks value-before-signup, guest expiry and channel-specific openings.

The suite includes an in-process end-to-end journey and server-route authorization checks; it is not clinical validation, privacy certification or an integration test against real social, model, identity or clinic services. The requirement-by-requirement results and deployment gaps are recorded in [TEST_RESULTS.md](TEST_RESULTS.md). Additional release tests should cover false negatives across languages and paraphrases, token theft/replay, concurrency during conversion, deliverability, queue acknowledgement, retention deletion jobs and marketing non-contact without consent.

## Next-week release decision

Before a real clinic pilot: (1) obtain clinical owner approval for triage rules and emergency copy; (2) replace demo auth with delivered verification and staff SSO; (3) validate redaction and external-model data handling or keep all answers local; (4) deploy behind TLS with managed keys, backups and scheduled retention; (5) integrate and load-test one approved acquisition channel; (6) add staffed escalation alerts, acknowledgement and response tracking; and (7) complete a privacy/security review using synthetic and adversarial test data. Until these gates pass, the responsible release is a supervised internal demonstration only.
